What you're looking at

Two ideas about memory, arguing until they agree.

Every brain has (at least) two ways of holding onto things: it can remember because two things happened together, or it can remember because of exactly when they happened. M7 is built from both, on purpose.

The coral core works like a memory that stores things by association — a smell that brings back a whole afternoon. In the field this is called a Hopfield network: it's very good at recognizing something it has felt before, even from a partial or noisy version of it.

The gold ring works like a memory that pays attention to timing — knowing when something is worth holding onto and when it's safe to let go. That's an HGRN, a gate that decides, moment to moment, how much of the past to carry forward.

On their own, each is lopsided: the associative memory is much better at some senses than others, and the timing memory can't tell you what something was, only when it mattered. M7's real contribution is small and specific — a learned gate that lets the two argue over every new experience, and blends their answer instead of picking a winner. That blend is what you see moving between the core and the ring: a live disagreement, being resolved, thousands of times a second.

Read the technical version
M7 (Spike-JEPA) is a tri-modal, modality-adaptive spiking neural network combining Hopfield associative memory with HGRN temporal gating, tuned by supervised contrastive learning and an embedding replay buffer inspired by hippocampal indexing theory. The gate learns to weight Hopfield vs. HGRN per modality, correcting a measured ~21.53pp asymmetry where Hopfield underperforms on auditory input relative to visual.
